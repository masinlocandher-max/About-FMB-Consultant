I do not expect the woman I become later to agree with every sentence I wrote here. I hope she has learned enough to correct me too.

So take this book seriously if it helps you. Argue with it when it does not. Listen to the people who have earned the right to tell you the truth. Pay attention to consequences. Change your mind when better information arrives.

Para sa sarili, so you do not disappear.

Para sa kapwa, so your success does not make your world too small.

Para sa bayan, so love of country becomes more than something you feel when the anthem plays.

Hindi ako sagabal sa ambisyon ninuman.

Build your life.

Pursue your work.

Become excellent at what deserves your excellence.

Help when you can.

Speak when it matters.

But make no mistake: peace is not indifference, and knowing yourself does not remove your responsibility to other people.

When your participation genuinely matters, do not let convenience be the reason you disappear.

I will not stand idly.

Then return to your own center and keep going.

The right way to live?

I can tell you mine.

You still have to discover yours.

# A NOTE ON THREE RETAINED LINES

The phrases “Hindi ako sagabal sa ambisyon ninuman,” “But make no mistake,” and “I will not stand idly” are retained as an acknowledgment of language used by President Gloria Macapagal-Arroyo in her State of the Nation Address delivered on July 23, 2007.

In that address, the lines referred to political ambition, national interest, and the national vision. In this book, they become points of departure for a personal philosophy about ambition, responsibility, citizenship, and refusing indifference when one’s participation matters.

The surrounding arguments and applications are original to this work.
